AI Board Direction: user guide

The app takes a board from “what AI do we use?” to a resolved position on AI: where it wants AI, what it will not accept, what must come back to it, what information may go where, what harm it will not tolerate, what it expects to hear, and who is accountable.

1

What the app is for

The board works through ten templated steps, B0 to B9, usually in one workshop of about three hours, and resolves the result at its next meeting. The board advisor facilitates, the general counsel (in-house or fractional) acts as counsel, and Triodian supplies the structure and records.

The engagement runs in one of two modes, chosen at set-up. Standalone, for companies outside prudential regulation, produces the whole appetite: an AI risk appetite statement the board resolves. Connected, for APRA, ASIC or MAS regulated entities, produces board directions that the AI Risk Appetite engagement must stay within; the board approves that engagement’s statement afterwards. The mode is fixed once the inventory has a line.

Two standing rules.The appetite is the board’s: the app records what the board decides and who proposed it, and never says what the position should be. Anything that turns on what the company is obliged to do is a question for counsel, logged in writing and not settled in the room. The app holds no client data or personal information: uses and information are described by kind.

2

Before you start

Sign in with your work email, or with the demonstration account you were given. An administrator adds each person in Admin and gives them one or more roles. What you can change depends on your roles; the server checks every save against the same rules the screens show, and a control you cannot use says why.

The app opens in the worked example: Harrow Valley Foods, a fictional company with its board and advisers, worked through every step. You can try every screen in it, including the inventory, the positions and the director briefing. Nothing in it is saved. Choose Leave the worked example in the band at the top to reach your own engagement; the menu (☰ → Practice) brings the example back.

The Switch app button opens the other Triodian apps. In connected mode the order is the AI Obligations Atlas, this app, AI Risk Appetite, then AI Use Assessment.

Codes such as A3, G6 or BM2 explain themselves: hover over one, or move to it with the keyboard.

B0 Set-upWhat applies to us, and what do directors need to know first?
B1 Where AI is usedWhat AI do we use now, directly and through providers?
B2 PostureWhere do we want AI to create value, and how cautious are we in each area? (With the governance goals.)
B3 Red linesWhich uses of AI do we not accept at all?
B4 ReservedWhich AI decisions must come back to us?
B5 Confidential informationWhat information may go into which AI tools?
B6 HarmWhat failure would be unacceptable to customers, staff or the company?

Each step opens to show its question, what is recorded, and, where you can record, the screen for it. Every step from B2 to B8 shows Prompts for the room and its starting position: a place to begin the discussion, never a recommendation.

5

Set-up, the brief and the briefing (B0)

Set-up

Triodian or an administrator records the company, entity type, jurisdiction, mode, areas of the business, the privilege marking counsel directs, the practitioner’s firm and logo (shown on every output), and the workshop and resolution dates.

The matter brief

Build a matter brief in the AI Obligations Atlas for the company’s entity type and jurisdiction, download it as JSON, and load it at B0 (Choose the brief file, or paste it). The obligations in scope appear by tier: Binding, Expected and Anticipated. The tiers describe the sources; counsel decides what applies. The questions counsel will need to determine can be logged to counsel in one step. A brief built for another jurisdiction or entity type is flagged. The lawyer’s matter label is not kept.

Agreement to the scope

The GC, then the chair (or the second approver), each record their agreement in their own name. If the brief is loaded again afterwards, the agreement is flagged so it can be given again. You can withdraw an agreement you recorded.

The director briefing

Every director takes the briefing before the workshop: AI in plain terms, the obligations in scope, how directors’ duties bear on AI (as questions for counsel), common ways AI fails, the ten steps and two scales, then four questions that check understanding. It takes about fifteen minutes. Choosing an answer shows the explanation; it is a check, not a test.

Who sees your answers.Only you and the facilitators (the board advisor and Triodian). The engagement records only the date you confirmed reading it and the date you completed it. Other directors, the chair and the GC do not see your answers. Step B9 lists any director who has not completed the briefing, and the chair decides whether to proceed.

The briefing downloads as a Word pre-read, and the director education record (read and completion dates, no answers) downloads for the company secretary.

6

The AI inventory (B1)

The CEO, or Triodian for them, records one line per AI use, usually 10 to 20 lines, with ten fields: the use, the tool and provider, the area, who uses it, its authority (A1 to A6), the highest class of information it sees (G1 to G6), the tool kind (K1 to K7), whether it decides about individuals, its owner, and the source. A use nobody can describe is recorded as not known, never left off. Lines with a field missing are marked Incomplete.

Decisions about individuals. Ticking field 8 records that the use makes, or substantially contributes to, a decision about a person using their personal information. It raises a question to counsel and puts the line on the automated-decision worklist for the GC, with the kinds of decision and the personal information used. The worklist downloads as Word. It records facts only; whether the Privacy Act requirements apply is counsel’s determination.

The inventory downloads as CSV.

7

Recording positions (B2 to B8)

In the workshop the steps are taken in order, and each position is tested against a line of the inventory before it is recorded. Each item is a block: the wording at full width, then the short fields. Every position records who proposed it and any dissent.

B2 Posture
First the governance goals: what AI governance should achieve, each with an owner and a measure that B7 reports. Then a posture for each area, from Avoid to Pursue, with any condition. Each area shows its starting position from the inventory, with Use this. A posture the room cannot settle is recorded as Not set, with who will set it and by when.
B3 Red lines
Each red line names the use, its scope and who it applies to, and is tested against the inventory: name the line that crosses it, or record that none does now. The uses boards commonly consider are shown only if the board asks.
B4 Reserved
Start from the six events RV1 to RV6, or add your own. Each records whether the board approves before or is told after, and who brings it.
B5 Confidential information
For each class of information and kind of tool: P permitted, C with conditions (written below the table), or N not permitted. Moving down a column a marking may stay or tighten; a marking that loosens is flagged at once.
B6 Harm
For each kind of harm, what the board will not tolerate and the threshold, preferably an existing one. Together they define a material AI incident.
B7 Reporting
Start from BM1 to BM5 and EV1 to EV4, or add your own. A governance goal’s measure can be added here and stays linked to its goal.
B8 Accountability
The committee, the lead director, the accountable executive, the owner of each area’s positions, and who may approve an exception.

The board advisor and the GC see every position but do not edit them.

8

Questions to counsel

Every step from B1 to B8 has Log a question to counsel. Questions are numbered QC-01 onward and go to the GC by email. The GC answers them under Your actions at the top of the engagement, and Triodian is told. Counsel can still answer questions after the board resolves; the minute lists any still open.

9

The checks

Checks before the outputs leave draft runs the seven checks of the framework as you record: BK1 complete, BK2 ordered, BK3 consistent, BK4 testable, BK5 owned, BK6 covered and BK7 referred. A failing check lists each item to resolve, with a link to its step. Failures go back to the CEO and the chair; Triodian does not resolve them.

The checks never stop the board resolving. They show what is incomplete or inconsistent, so the board decides knowingly. The reality note is separate: inventory lines that already cross a position (above an area’s posture, crossing a red line, or using information marked N). It is not a failure; each line goes on the 90-day action list.

10

Resolving (B9) and versions

Before the meeting, B9 shows what the chair should see: steps with nothing recorded, checks not passing, questions open, directors yet to complete the briefing, and the reality note. The chair drafts the resolution (it saves as it is written), any amendments made at the meeting, the minute reference, the review interval and the change events that bring the positions back. Record the resolution asks for a second click, because it locks the engagement: positions and the inventory can no longer change.

Versions. A version is issued at each sign-off: when the scope is agreed (B0) and when the board resolves (B9). Each keeps the areas, the inventory and the positions as they were. Under Versions in B9, Compare with now lists what has changed since. A resolved output’s status names the version, for example “Resolved by the board, version V2”.

The next review. When the review interval passes or a change event occurs, the method owner or an administrator chooses Start the next review. The resolved engagement is kept read-only; the inventory and positions carry over as starting positions for the board to confirm, and questions, the briefing and the resolution start afresh.

Outputs

OutputFor
AI risk appetite statement (standalone)The board, to resolve. Ten sections, opening with the governance goals.
Board directions paper (connected)The board, then the AI Risk Appetite engagement. Directions BD1 to BD7.
Acceptable-use policy outline (standalone)The GC, to draft the staff policy. It is not a policy.
Board minuteThe company secretary.
90-day action listThe CEO: every gap between the inventory and the positions, with an owner and a date.
Automated-decision worklistThe GC.
Engagement planEveryone: the six steps, with dates, who, and where each stands.
Director education recordThe chair and the company secretary.
Confidentiality position file (JSON, standalone)A later confidential-matter assessment, which shows a suggested required rung on each channel for counsel to confirm.

Every Word output carries the privilege marking on its first line and in the page header, and its status in the footer of every page: “Draft, not resolved by the board” until the chair records the resolution. Outputs state what was recorded and who proposed it. They never say what the law requires or what the board should decide, and they name no product.

12

Email notifications and your profile

A save that gives someone something to do sends them an email:

  • A question to counsel goes to the GC; counsel’s answer goes to Triodian.
  • The scope: one agreement tells the other party (the chair, or the second approver, and the GC).
  • A step waiting for you: once the scope is agreed, the CEO hears that the inventory is next, and each director that their briefing is ready.
  • The resolution recorded goes to everyone with a role.

And once a day, at most once per person:

  • the GC, while any question to counsel has been open five days or more;
  • in the three days before the workshop, each director who has not completed the briefing, and the CEO while the inventory is empty or incomplete;
  • in the week before the resolution meeting, the chair and Triodian: how many checks pass, how many questions are open and how many directors have not completed the briefing.

Emails carry step names, references and counts only: no positions and no inventory detail. You can turn them off, and change your name, organisation and email address, under Your profile.
